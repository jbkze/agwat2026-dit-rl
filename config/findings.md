**1. Rewarding only the yield at the end of the season works better than the stepwise reward of DRL1.** Changing only the reward raises test yield by {{terminal@nebraska 75}} in Nebraska at 75 mm and by {{terminal@tunis 300}} in Tunis at 300 mm. At every decision the stepwise reward adds the relative evapotranspiration (actual over potential) of a single day, the last day of the 5-day interval. On sandy soil that day is fully wet only if the agent irrigates at every decision, so in Tunis DRL1 gives 34 mm every 5 days while the canopy grows, empties the budget before flowering and grain filling, and the crop dies. The reward for a wet root zone comes at once, the cost of an empty budget weeks later: rerun with the stepwise reward as score, the policies trained on the yield alone even earn a higher stepwise return, so DRL1 settles on a policy that is worse by its own measure. In Nebraska the summed ratios are mostly set by the weather and make up about two thirds of the return, which leaves little weight for the yield. In Tunis at 150 and 200 mm the crop has died in every run with the stepwise reward so far, in every combination of the other two design choices. Only with a large budget (Tunis 500 mm, {{terminal@tunis 500}}) do both rewards apply the water at nearly the same time and reach nearly the same yield.

**2. The 18 observed variables of Kelly et al. (2024) help in Nebraska and hurt in Tunis.** In Nebraska they raise test yield by {{obs@nebraska 75}} at 75 mm, in Tunis they lower it by {{obs@tunis 500}} at 500 mm. Nebraska has rain and weather that differs from year to year, which the extra variables can describe. Tunis is simulated without rain, so there they plausibly add mostly noise. The 18 variables are given to the network without scaling, and the experiment with scaled variables, which has not run yet, will show whether part of the effect comes from the scaling.

**3. How the budget is enforced matters less than the reward, and its effect depends on the case.** Rejecting a dose that exceeds the remaining budget, where DRL1 masks it, changes test yield by {{reject@nebraska 75}} in Nebraska at 75 mm and by {{reject@tunis 300}} in Tunis at 300 mm. Adding a penalty to the rejection helps in Tunis at 300 mm ({{penalty@tunis 300}}) but costs yield at 600 mm ({{penalty@tunis 600}}). Masking, the choice of DRL1, is therefore not better than the alternatives.
